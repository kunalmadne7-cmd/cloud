CookieCo: Profitability Case 🍪

BCG-style mock interview · Interviewer: Jenny Rae Le Roux (ex-Bain) · Candidate: Edward

1. Case Prompt

Client = CookieCo: national manufacturer + seller of packaged cookies in the US.

Last 2–3 yrs: profits ↓ declining … but rest of industry = healthy growth ↑

Ask: find the ROOT CAUSE + how to REVERSE it.

2. Clarifying Qs (Ed asked)

  • Scope = only US? → Yes ✔
  • Only cookies, or other packaged goods too? → They sell other stuff, but focus = cookie segment only
  • "Can I take a few mins to structure?" → Go for it
💡 Feedback: good Qs, but say WHY you're asking, e.g. "outside the US the supply chain gets much more complex, so I'll assume US only." Don't rush into the structure: be in command, not hurried.

3. How the interview flowed

Recap→ Clarify Qs→ Structure→ Hypothesis: Rev or Cost?→ Brainstorm costs→ Exhibit 1→ Storage breakdown→ Fix inventory loss→ Final rec→ Feedback

BCG style = candidate-led, conversational. Interviewer: "If there are numbers or exhibits, it's up to you to put them into your structure." Expect to pivot as new info comes in!

4. Ed's Framework

Structure score 3/4. To get a 4: ① lead with costs (hypothesis-driven: "here's what I think the issue is", not a generic profit tree). ② Don't stay "high level": add depth, e.g. revenue → product mix, discounting, inflation impact. ③ Keep buckets MECE: don't let "market" overlap with revenue.

5. Hypothesis: Revenue or Cost?

Market growing + competitors growing ⇒ we're probably selling fine → margin squeeze ⇒ root cause = COSTS

Feedback: should have said this up-front, during the recap! "Others are doing well → is it a cost issue? (inflation + strong consumer-goods demand) → probably costs."

6. Costs along the "life of a cookie"

🏭 Make (ingredients, factory)→ 📦 Store→ 🎁 Package→ 🚚 Distribute (drivers, freight)

Macro context (2020–22)

  • Pandemic + recession → labour supply ↓ (drivers, freight operators) → supply & demand → distribution at a premium
  • But distribution hits the whole market, not just us. Hmm 🤔 doesn't explain why only we are down
  • Ed's #1 pick before data: distribution (also wanted raw-material data)

7. Exhibit 1: Cost segments, last 3 yrs ($M)

Reading it left → right (values ≈, read off a low-res video)

  • Raw materials: steady, ~linear ↑. Expected (inflation, labour, more demand for ingredients as industry grows). Not scary.
  • Storage & warehousing: biggest increase by far → focus here
  • Distribution: flat 1 yr, then small ↑ (2022 could be a macro anomaly) → distribution pivot away
  • SG&A: flat. Mktg & promo: flat, then a small ↑

🔍 Insight: distribution ↑ + raw materials ↑ could mean we're shipping more product ⇒ revenue likely growing ⇒ confirms the cost hypothesis.

⚠ Math feedback: Ed said "12% → 22%", but it's $ millions, NOT %! State units first, then do the % math out loud for the interviewer.

8. Drill down: Storage & Warehousing

"If we built an Excel sheet, what are the sub-components?"

  • 🏢 Rent of the warehouse
  • 🌡️ Climate control (not hot/humid)
  • 👷 Labour moving stock (like Amazon)
  • 🗑️ Inventory loss: cookies EXPIRE

How does expired stock hit the P&L? You pay ~all the cost (make + store, maybe not distribution) and get $0 revenue ⇒ pure negative profit.

NEW INFO Inventory losses have spiked in the last 2–3 yrs!

9. How to bring inventory losses under control

(Ed asked for 20 sec to organize thoughts ✔)

Root causeFix
① Bad storage conditionsClimate control (humidity, temp). caveat: packaging should protect too
② Poor inventory mgmt (boxes forgotten at the back)Proper mgmt system + FIFO: first in, first out LIFO
③ Over-production / low turnoverDemand analytics + forecasting, so we don't over-make in a recession
④ Product shelf-life too shortOptimize recipe / packaging for freshness
⚠ Creative feedback (3/4): question was "suggest ways to bring them under control" = SOLUTIONS, but Ed first listed problems. Answer the question asked. And WRITE the brainstorm down: don't cap the pen!

10. Final Recommendation (elevator pitch to CEO)

Ed's version (2.5/4): storage & warehousing costs too high → fix climate + mgmt systems → risk: spending more without finding the issue

✨ Better version (story format + numbered, as the feedback asks)

Context: CookieCo's profits are falling while the market & competitors grow.

Finding: it's a cost problem. Storage & warehousing rose ~$12M → ~$20M in 2 yrs, driven by inventory losses (expired cookies).

Recs: 1) FIFO inventory-mgmt system 2) climate-controlled storage 3) better demand forecasting

Risks: 1) upfront spend before savings show 2) forecasting errors. Next steps: 1) audit warehouses 2) size the loss by SKU

12. Key Takeaways ⭐

  • Profitability case + growing market ⇒ state the hypothesis early (likely costs)
  • Give context for every clarifying Q. Slow down: be in command, not rushing
  • Structure: hypothesis-led, go deep (not "high level"), keep it MECE
  • Exhibits: read the units first ($M ≠ %), then do the % math out loud
  • Answer the exact question (solutions ≠ causes)
  • Write brainstorms down; keep the pen ready, esp. in BCG (it's conversational, you may not know when you're moving on)
  • Final rec: 1-line context → finding → numbered recs → numbered risks / next steps
  • Rec is verbal; no need to write it. Notes are for the brainstorming parts
